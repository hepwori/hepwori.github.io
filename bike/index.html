model & assumptions

everything is a steady-state power balance: at constant speed, what the rider puts into the cranks (minus drivetrain loss) equals the power burned against gravity, rolling resistance, and air.

η·P = v · [ m·g·(sinθ + Crr·cosθ) + ½·ρ·CdA·(v + w)·|v + w| ]

in "power → speed" mode the page solves this for v (largest root, so tailwinds behave). in "speed → power" mode it's just evaluated. cadence then falls out of speed and gear: cadence = v / (wheel circumference × chainring/cog), and torque is P / ω.

solid physics is standard and fine · good enough simplified, small or bounded error · crude don't trust in that regime

how the slice works

the amber plane cuts the surface at a fixed value of one axis variable (drag its bars, use the slider above the chart, or click the chart). the 2d chart is exactly the curve where plane meets surface, with any two outputs on left and right axes. in gear + cadence mode, hold cadence and the x axis is gear ratio: that's power and speed across every gear at one cadence. with "snap to real gears" the marker jumps between actual ring/cog combos.
